In this study we develop a surrogate model that uses varying cycle lengths and operational parameters. The surrogate model can accurately predict hydrogen saturation and pressure fields with maximum error rates of 1.6\% and 0.26\% respectively. Injection rate and hydrogen stored predictions are highly accurate with errors of 5.54\% and 0.649\%. Autoregressive rollout predictions are shown to be accurate for forecasting the injection rate, hydrogen stored and pressure for the maximum injection cycle length seen in the natural gas data. Compared to previous studies, this research assesses key operational parameters more thoroughly. The implications of this study are that: (1) underground hydrogen storage surrogate models need to use varying cycle lengths and operational rates if they are to be operated in a similar way to natural gas storage, (2) future operators have a framework to develop an accurate surrogate model which can forecast the impacts of different injection cycle lengths and rates and (3) future research has a proposed solution to improve prediction of hydrogen withdrawal rates and reduce compute required.
